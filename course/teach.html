The promise to students

"If you follow along and can explain every decision in this project, you will have concrete evidence for a broad range of Snowflake / dbt / SQL / data-modelling interviews." That goal is supported by three rules used throughout:

  • Nothing is theoretical. Every concept is followed by a query, a number, or a failing test.
  • Every "best practice" is shown broken first. Students remember the fan-out, the seq scan, the spilled sort — not the slide.
  • Decisions are explained with trade-offs, never "because that's how it's done".
Analogy used throughout the course
The bank's Postgres is the kitchen where food is cooked (OLTP, optimised for many small writes). The warehouse is the restaurant floor where people sit and eat (OLAP, optimised for big reads). Ingestion is the waiter carrying plates every few minutes. dbt is the plating station that arranges raw ingredients into dishes (dims and facts). Airflow is the head chef's clock that calls "service!" on schedule. Nobody eats in the kitchen; nobody cooks on the restaurant floor.

Meridian Bank (fictional)

Footprint
520 branches — India 300, UAE 60, UK 60, USA 60, Singapore 40
Currencies
INR, AED, GBP, USD, SGD. Group reports in USD (one dbt variable, reporting_currency)
Core system
PostgreSQL 16, normalised schema: customers, accounts, joint holders, transactions, loan applications, branches, employees, products, FX rates
Volumes (seeded)
200k customers, 300k accounts, 5M historical transactions (scalable to 20M+), ~1.5k FX rows, 60k loan applications; simulator adds ~100 transactions/second on demand
Asks from the business
  1. Branch and group KPIs refreshed every 5 minutes (deposits, withdrawals, net flow, fee income, digital adoption)
  2. All money in local currency and USD, converted correctly for the date it happened
  3. History must be true: a customer who was RETAIL in March and PREMIUM in June must be counted as RETAIL for March
  4. Month-end balances (stock) and daily flows both available
  5. Loan pipeline time-to-decision trends
  6. Auditability: what did the source say at 10:03 yesterday?
Constraints
Free Snowflake trial, laptop Postgres, no paid SaaS connectors. Source team allows read access + triggers, nothing else.

Why not just query Postgres?

Because OLTP and OLAP want opposite things
OLTP (Postgres)OLAP (Snowflake)
Typical queryfetch 1 account, insert 1 transactionsum 50M rows by month and region
Storagerow-oriented; one row = one block readcolumnar; reads only the columns asked
Optimised forlatency of a single write; indexes; ACIDthroughput of big scans; pruning; compression
Schemanormalised (no duplication, many joins)denormalised star (few joins, readable)
Dangerone analyst's 10-minute query locks tellers outnone — compute is separate from the bank
Non-functional requirements we design against
  • Latency: ≤ 15 minutes p95 source → mart, delivered by a 5-minute micro-batch. Not seconds: only fraud would benefit, and the design doc prices the upgrade instead of paying for it on day one.
  • Correctness: late updates (PENDING→POSTED), hard deletes, FX restatements, weekends with no rates.
  • History: SCD2 on customer and account; append-only raw layer keeps every version.
  • Cost: auto-suspend warehouses, resource monitor, incremental models, separate warehouses per workload.
  • Operability: control tables, freshness SLAs, reconciliation, alerts via Airflow.

Why this and not that
That sentence is an ADR, not a preference. Nine of them are in 00_architecture/adr/. Read the rejected column, not only the decision. ADR 0003 is why Kimball and not Data Vault. ADR 0009 is why "find part of a transaction id" is a serving index and not Elasticsearch.

0.1 Requirements are numbers (DESIGN_DOC.md §2)

"Fast", "reliable" and "secure" are not requirements. "p95 freshness of hot facts ≤ 15 minutes, measured by dbt source freshness every 15 minutes" is. Every NFR in the design doc has a target, a way to measure it, and a consequence if missed. That last column is what turns a wish list into a design: it tells you which requirement is worth paying for.

Analogy
A builder asked for "a big, strong house" builds whatever they like. A builder given "four bedrooms, 2,000 sq ft, survives a magnitude-6 earthquake, budget ₹1.2 crore" can say no to a marble staircase. Numbers give you the right to say no.

0.2 Capacity and cost: do the arithmetic out loud (§4)

Storage is not the problem. 2M transactions a day at about 250 bytes, compressed about 5×, is about 100 MB a day. Compute is the problem, and the micro-batch is the expensive part:

288 runs/day × (90 s work + 60 s auto-suspend idle) = 43,200 s = 12 XS-hours = 12 credits/day
≈ 360 credits/month  ≈ $1,080/month at an assumed $3/credit (check edition, cloud, region)

Now the architect's move: the 5-minute cadence is justified for authorisations and transactions (fraud). It is not justified for ref.countries. Moving reference tables to the daily DAG removes most tables from the hot path. Write the saving in an ADR. An interviewer who hears that sentence knows you have run a platform, not just built one.

"What we deliberately did not build" (§7)
Data Vault, streaming everywhere, a feature-store product, Iceberg. Each has a sentence explaining why not and what would change the answer. Knowing what not to build is the most senior skill in this course, and the cheapest to demonstrate.

0.4 Failure modes before features (§6)

For every component: how does it fail, how do we detect it, and what limits the blast radius? The table in the design doc maps each answer to a file: idempotent re-runs, tombstones for deletes, the FX forward-fill flag, the contract on dim_branch, the AUC gate, the governance post-hook that fails closed, the resource monitor.

Snowflake trial choices that matter

  • Edition: Enterprise. Standard lacks Search Optimization, Materialized Views, multi-cluster warehouses, and limits Time Travel to 1 day.
  • Cloud/region: AWS, closest region (ap-south-1 Mumbai for India). Irrelevant for the course, but PUT latency is lower.
  • Auth: humans use MFA; service users use key-pair. The scripts show both. Password-only service users are being deprecated — teach key-pair from day one.
  • Timezone: ALTER ACCOUNT SET TIMEZONE = 'UTC'. The whole platform speaks UTC; local time is a presentation concern (dim_branch carries the branch timezone).

1.3 Making change visible: the two CDC hooks (04_cdc_triggers.sql)

Our ingestion asks Postgres one question every 5 minutes: "what changed since :watermark?" → WHERE updated_at > :wm. For that to be trustworthy the source must guarantee two things, and applications routinely break both:

1. updated_at is always bumped

A BEFORE UPDATE trigger sets NEW.updated_at := clock_timestamp(). Note clock_timestamp(), not now() — now() is frozen at transaction start, so a long transaction would stamp an old time on a late commit.

2. Hard deletes are recorded

A watermark query cannot see a row that no longer exists. An AFTER DELETE trigger writes (schema, table, pk, deleted_at) into cdc.deleted_records. One generic function serves every table by receiving the PK column name as TG_ARGV[0] and using EXECUTE format('SELECT ($1).%I', …).

Interview
"How do you capture deletes with a timestamp-based CDC?" — soft-delete flag, delete-log trigger (ours), periodic full-key reconciliation, or move to log-based CDC. "What about rows updated during a long transaction?" — the extractor's INGEST_LAG_SECONDS safety window plus re-merge lookback.

2.1 The Kimball four-step process, applied live

  1. Select the business process. Not a department, not a report: a process that generates measurements. Ours: account transactions, month-end balances, loan applications, FX publication.
  2. Declare the grain. Say it as a sentence. "One row in fct_transactions is one money movement on one account at its latest known status." Everything else follows from the grain; most modelling bugs are grain bugs (mixing daily totals with individual transactions).
  3. Identify the dimensions. Ask "who, what, where, when, how" of the grain: date, customer, account, branch, product, channel, currency, type/status, employee.
  4. Identify the facts. Numeric, additive measures true to the grain: amount_local, amount_reporting, signed variants, count. Non-additive things (rates, balances) are handled with care (semi-additive).
Analogy
A fact table is a till receipt: lines of numbers (facts) with codes pointing at the catalogue (dimensions). Dimensions are the catalogue pages: descriptive, wordy, few rows, many columns. You filter and group by catalogue pages; you add up receipt lines.

A transaction on 2026-05-01 joins to version 2: transaction_ts >= valid_from AND transaction_ts < valid_to. That predicate is the scd2_match() macro. The fact stores the version-specific surrogate key, so BI tools never need the range predicate again.

Two SCD2 details that separate a textbook from a working warehouse
Backdating: the first version's valid_from is set to 1900-01-01. Without that, a transaction from before the first snapshot run matches no version and falls to the unknown member.
Open-ended valid_to = 9999-12-31, not NULL: ts < NULL is UNKNOWN and silently drops rows; a real date keeps the predicate simple and sargable.
Interview rapid fire
Type 0 keep original (date of birth). Type 1 overwrite (branch name). Type 2 add a row (segment). Type 3 add a "previous value" column (previous segment only). Type 4 mini-dimension for fast-changing attributes (risk score bands). Type 6 = 1+2+3 hybrid (Type 2 rows that also carry the current value). Surrogate vs natural key: surrogate is stable, compact, version-specific, insulates from source key reuse/merges. Unknown member: row −1 so facts never have NULL FKs and INNER JOINs never lose rows.

2.4 The three fact table types (we build all three)

Transaction fact — fct_transactions

One row per event. Densest, most flexible. Fully additive measures. Grows forever → incremental.

Periodic snapshot — fct_monthly_account_balance

One row per entity per period, whether or not anything happened. Semi-additive balance (add across accounts, never across months). Answers "what was the book on 31 March?" instantly.

Accumulating snapshot — fct_loan_applications

One row per process instance, updated in place as milestones happen. Many date keys + lag measures (days_to_decision). Rebuilt or merged.

Additivity
Additive: amount — sum across every dimension. Semi-additive: balance — sum across accounts, but take the last value across time. Non-additive: FX rate, ratios — never sum; average with care or recompute from numerator/denominator.

2.5 Patterns students must be able to name

Conformed dimension
Same dim, same keys, used by several facts → drill-across. dim_branch serves transactions, balances, loans, aggregates.
Degenerate dimension
A dimension with no attributes other than its key, kept on the fact: transaction_id, reference_no.
Junk dimension
Several low-cardinality flags/codes combined into one small dim: dim_transaction_type (type × status).
Role-playing dimension
One physical dim used in several roles: dim_date as applied/approved/disbursed date in fct_loan_applications.
Bridge table
Resolves M:N; weighting factor lets totals reconcile (allocated view) or show full impact (double-counted view, on purpose).
Factless fact
A fact with no measures (e.g., customer-eligible-for-product). fct_fx_rates_daily is close: one measure, mostly used for lookup.
Late-arriving dimension
A fact references a customer the warehouse has not seen yet → unknown member now, repointed when the dim arrives (our 5-minute dim refresh makes this rare).
Star vs snowflake
We denormalise country into dim_branch and product into dim_account (star). Snowflaking (normalised dims) saves bytes and costs joins; columnar compression makes the saving irrelevant.
One Big Table (OBT) / Data Vault
Mention: OBT = pre-joined star for a specific BI tool (we have agg_branch_daily_performance); Data Vault = hubs/links/satellites for raw integration at enterprise scale, with a star on top — our append-only VARIANT raw plays the "keep everything" role cheaply.

2.6 Multi-currency done properly

  1. Store both. Every money fact row carries amount_local, amount_reporting (USD) and fx_rate_to_reporting. Never convert at query time with today's rate — history would move every day.
  2. Flows at transaction-date rate, stocks at period-end rate. Transactions convert on the day they happened; month-end balances convert at the month-end rate. Summing converted flows ≠ converting the summed balance — that difference is the FX translation effect and finance expects to see it (fct_monthly_account_balance exposes both).
  3. No gaps. Rates exist on business days only; a Saturday card payment uses Friday's close (forward fill, flagged with fx_is_forward_filled and rate_age_days, tested ≤ 5 days).
  4. Restatements. Treasury may correct yesterday's close; staging keeps the latest version; downstream recompute naturally.
  5. Changing the reporting currency is one variable and a --full-refresh.

3.1 Architecture in three layers

Cloud servicesParser, optimizer, metadata, security, transactions, result cache. Always on; free up to 10% of daily compute.
Compute — virtual warehousesIndependent clusters of compute. Start/stop per second. Each has its own local SSD cache. Multiple warehouses read the same data with zero contention.
Storage — micro-partitionsImmutable, columnar, compressed files of 50–500 MB (uncompressed), each with min/max metadata per column. Pruning = skipping partitions by metadata.
Why this matters for everything else
Compute is billed per second per warehouse size (XS = 1 credit/h, each size doubles). Storage is cheap (~$23/TB/month) and separate. So: small warehouses that stop quickly, queries that prune, and incremental builds are the three levers of cost — which is exactly what the perf lab measures.

Why VARIANT and append-only?
  • Schema drift: a new column in Postgres lands tomorrow without a DDL change; staging decides when to expose it.
  • Idempotent: re-running a batch appends; staging dedupes by qualify row_number() over (partition by pk order by _extracted_at desc) = 1.
  • Audit: every version of every row is kept — Time Travel for free, forever.
  • Cheap: columnar compression of semi-structured data is excellent; Snowflake also auto-extracts VARIANT sub-columns into their own columnar storage, so record:amount::number prunes reasonably.
Trade-off to say out loud: casting in staging costs CPU on every run; a typed landing table is faster to query but brittle. We keep VARIANT and pay for it in the staging views, then measure.

Stage & file format. One internal stage per source schema (MERIDIAN_RAW.POSTGRES_CORE.PG_STAGE, …POSTGRES_REF.PG_STAGE, …POSTGRES_CDC.PG_STAGE) and FF_PARQUET (USE_LOGICAL_TYPE = TRUE so Parquet decimals/timestamps become NUMBER/TIMESTAMP, not strings). Control tables: CONTROL.WATERMARKS (one row per table), CONTROL.LOAD_LOG (one row per table per batch), views V_PIPELINE_HEALTH (lag per table, rows in last hour) and V_LOAD_THROUGHPUT.

The transactions raw table is CLUSTER BY (_extracted_at) — micro-batches arrive in time order anyway, so this costs almost nothing and makes "give me the last 2 hours" prune perfectly.

4.2 One batch for one table, step by step

1 · get_watermark read CONTROL.WATERMARKS; default 1900-01-01 → first run is a full load. One MERGE at the end updates it.
2 · fix the window high = now() − INGEST_LAG_SECONDS. The lag (10 s) lets in-flight transactions commit: updated_at is assigned when the row is written, but the row becomes visible only at commit — a row stamped 12:00:00 can commit at 12:00:04, after a batch that ran at 12:00:02 has already moved on.
3 · extract_table SELECT * FROM core.transactions WHERE updated_at > %s AND updated_at <= %s ORDER BY updated_at through a named (server-side) cursor with itersize so 5M rows never sit in memory; chunks of 200k rows become one Parquet file each.
4 · typed Parquet build an Arrow schema from the cursor description (Postgres OIDs → Arrow types) and add the metadata columns; pq.write_table(..., compression='snappy').
5 · PUT one wildcard PUT file:///…/core.transactions_*.parquet @POSTGRES_CORE.PG_STAGE/transactions/<batch>/ AUTO_COMPRESS=FALSE PARALLEL=8.
6 · COPY INTO transformational COPY: SELECT $1, '<extracted_at>', '<batch>', METADATA$FILENAME FROM @stage into the VARIANT table; PURGE = TRUE removes the files; ON_ERROR = ABORT_STATEMENT (never load half a batch).
7 · bookkeeping LOAD_LOG row (rows, files, seconds, status) and the watermark advanced to the max updated_at actually observed, not to high.
8 · deletes ride along: cdc.deleted_records is just another table in config.TABLES; dbt applies them (M5).
Why advance the watermark to max observed, not to "high"?
Both are defensible. Max observed is the safer teaching default: if the Postgres clock or a replica lags, you never skip rows that fall between the last observed and high. The cost is possibly re-reading the boundary row; the > comparison plus staging dedupe makes that harmless.
The dedupe contract
Because the raw layer is append-only and batches can overlap by design (lag window, retries), every staging model dedupes on the business PK, keeping the newest _extracted_at. Teach this as a contract between ingestion and transformation: "raw may contain duplicates; staging never does."

4.4 PUT and COPY mechanics worth knowing cold

  • File size. Snowflake loads one file per thread; 100–250 MB compressed is the sweet spot. Our 200k-row chunks ≈ 15–25 MB — slightly small on purpose so an XS warehouse shows parallelism; tune via INGEST_CHUNK_ROWS.
  • Load metadata. COPY remembers loaded file names for 64 days and will not reload them (FORCE=TRUE overrides). With PURGE=TRUE plus a batch id in the path this never bites.
  • Transformational COPY allows selecting columns/expressions and METADATA$FILENAME, METADATA$FILE_ROW_NUMBER, but not joins or aggregates.
  • VALIDATION_MODE = RETURN_ERRORS dry-runs a COPY — demo it once.
  • Why not write_pandas? It does the same PUT/COPY under the hood but creates typed tables from pandas dtypes — the exact type drift we want to avoid; and it hides the stage, which students must learn.

6.2 Design choices to say out loud

  • Airflow shells out; it does not transform. @task.bash runs python pg_to_snowflake.py --tables X and dbt build …. Logic lives in the tools that are testable on their own; Airflow adds schedule, retries (retries=2, retry_delay=30s), SLAs and logs.
  • Idempotency makes retries safe. Because the extractor's watermark only moves on success and raw is append-only + deduped, re-running any task is harmless. Say this: an orchestrator can only retry safely what was designed to be re-run.
  • Micro-batch vs daily split. Everything that must be fresh in 5 minutes is cheap (incremental). Everything expensive or history-sensitive (snapshots, SCD2 dims, month-end facts) runs nightly on WH_TRANSFORM_M.
  • Secrets via Airflow Connections/Variables or .env mounted by Astro — never in the DAG file.
  • Failure handling is operational. A shared structured callback records DAG/task/run context, and the health task fails instead of merely printing stale lag.

7.1 The nine experiments

01 · Postgres: the CDC query seq-scans the whole table — 01_postgres_missing_index.sql

Before: EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS) SELECT … WHERE updated_at > now() - interval '5 minutes' shows Parallel Seq Scan, "Rows Removed by Filter: 4,99x,xxx", shared read buffers in the hundreds of thousands, 1–3 s on 5M rows (worse as the simulator grows the table). After CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY ix_transactions_updated_at ON core.transactions (updated_at): Index/Bitmap Scan, a few hundred buffers, single-digit ms. Verified on PG14 at 1M rows.

Teach: CONCURRENTLY (no write lock on a live OLTP table); the trade-off (every UPDATE now maintains one more index — and our trigger bumps updated_at on every update, so the index churns; still worth it); the second missing index (account_id, transaction_ts) for the statement query in L03; pg_stat_user_tables.seq_scan to find such tables in production; track_io_timing for I/O time in the plan.

02 · Snowflake: micro-partition pruning and clustering — 02_snowflake_pruning_clustering.sql

Build PERF_LAB.TXN_RANDOM_ORDER: 100M synthetic rows via GENERATOR inserted in random date order, and TXN_SORTED: the same rows ORDER BY transaction_date. Query one week in each. Random: partitions_scanned ≈ partitions_total (every partition contains every date → nothing prunes), several seconds, GBs scanned. Sorted: a handful of partitions, sub-second, MBs scanned. Then ALTER TABLE TXN_RANDOM_ORDER CLUSTER BY (transaction_date), wait for automatic reclustering, rerun, and show SYSTEM$CLUSTERING_INFORMATION depth going from ~hundreds to single digits.

Teach: pruning is the Snowflake performance primitive; natural ordering at insert is free clustering; explicit clustering costs credits forever (automatic reclustering) and is only worth it for > TB tables with a dominant filter column; never cluster on a high-cardinality column directly (use date_trunc or trunc(id, -5)); our dbt facts use cluster_by transaction_date for this reason.

03 · Spilling and warehouse size — 03_spilling_and_warehouse_size.sql

A window function over 100M rows (row_number() over (partition by account_id order by transaction_ts) with a wide select) on XS: bytes_spilled_to_local_storage in GBs, maybe remote spill, minutes. Fix 1 — rewrite: project only needed columns, filter before the window, use qualify: spill drops dramatically on the same XS. Fix 2 — scale up to WH_TRANSFORM_M for the original query: ~4× faster at 4× the per-second cost, so roughly the same credits but less wall time.

Teach: spill = the operator's working set exceeded memory; local spill is bad, remote spill is terrible; bigger warehouses have more memory per node; "same credits, less time" is the scale-up rule of thumb when a query is memory-bound; rewriting is free.

04 · The SCD2 join explosion — 04_join_explosion_scd2.sql

Join transactions to dim_customer on customer_id only. Row count of the join > row count of the fact (every customer version matches). Deposits total is inflated by the average number of versions. Add {{ scd2_match() }} (ts >= valid_from and ts < valid_to) and the counts reconcile; assert_fct_transactions_reconciles_to_staging is the automated version of this check.

Teach: a fan-out is a correctness bug that looks like a performance bug (slower, more bytes, more rows) — always compare count(*) before and after a join to a dimension; half-open intervals avoid double-matching at version boundaries.

05 · The three caches — 05_caching_layers.sql

Run a 100M-row aggregate three times: cold (new warehouse, result cache off) → warm local disk cache (same warehouse, result cache off: faster, bytes scanned from cache) → result cache on (identical SQL within 24h: ~0 ms, 0 bytes, no warehouse credits). Then suspend the warehouse and show the local cache is gone. Then SELECT COUNT(*) and MIN/MAX on a column: metadata-only, no warehouse needed at all.

Teach: result cache (cloud services, 24h, exact text match, data unchanged, any warehouse), local disk cache (per warehouse, survives until suspend), metadata cache (count/min/max, free); why auto-suspend trades cache warmth for cost; why benchmarks must disable the result cache.

06 · Predicate anti-patterns — 06_predicate_antipatterns.sql

Pairs of queries with numbers: WHERE TO_DATE(transaction_ts) = '2026-03-01' vs WHERE transaction_ts >= '2026-03-01' AND transaction_ts < '2026-03-02' (function on the column defeats pruning on the sorted table); SELECT * vs named columns (columnar: bytes scanned scale with columns touched); UNION vs UNION ALL (hidden distinct sort); DISTINCT to "fix" a fan-out vs fixing the join; implicit casts (WHERE account_id = '123' on a NUMBER column); OR across columns vs UNION ALL; LIKE '%abc'.

Teach: sargable predicates; look at "bytes scanned" not only elapsed; how the optimizer's pruning works on min/max metadata only.

07 · Search Optimization and Materialized Views (Enterprise) — 07_search_optimization_and_mv.sql

a) Point lookup WHERE reference_no = 'a1b2…' on 100M rows sorted by date: a full scan (reference_no is random, pruning cannot help). ALTER TABLE … ADD SEARCH OPTIMIZATION ON EQUALITY(reference_no, transaction_id), wait for the build, rerun: partitions scanned collapse to one-digit, elapsed sub-second. a2) The same id, but only a fragment: CONTAINS(reference_no, fragment). Equality search optimization does not accelerate it. Add ON SUBSTRING(reference_no) and measure again. That is the access path behind omni_search. b) Daily currency totals: 100M-row aggregate vs CREATE MATERIALIZED VIEW MV_DAILY_CURRENCY_TOTALS — the optimizer rewrites the base-table query to hit the MV automatically.

Teach: SOS = a persistent search access path (think bloom filters per partition) for equality/IN/substring/geo lookups, costs storage + maintenance credits; MVs are single-table, auto-maintained, Enterprise; both are "pay credits to buy latency" — compare with simply clustering or building a dbt aggregate (agg_branch_daily_performance) which is free to query.

08 · dbt: full refresh vs incremental, and the wrong incremental filter — 08_dbt_incremental_vs_full_refresh.md

Run 1: dbt build -s fct_transactions --full-refresh every "5 minutes" — minutes of runtime, full-table scan, PIT joins over the whole history. Run 2: incremental — seconds, only the lookback window read, MERGE touches a few partitions. Why the first version of this design was wrong (show it, it is the lesson)
An earlier draft wrote scores into a Postgres table and let the extractor carry them back. Three problems, each an interview answer: (1) analytical output inside the system of record; (2) a loop — warehouse → model → OLTP → extractor → warehouse — where a bad batch replicates before anyone looks; (3) training/serving skew: pandas recomputed features from raw Postgres while the marts computed them differently in SQL. Fix: one dbt model defines every feature, training and scoring both read it, and scores land in the warehouse. 00_architecture/adr/0005.

Three guards make it safe to schedule unattended. Time-based holdouts: the latest 60 days of applications and 14 days of authorisations are the test set, never a random split, because the future must not leak into training. Release gate: below holdout AUC 0.70 (env MERIDIAN_AUC_GATE) the run is recorded as REJECTED and its scores are not published, so the marts keep the last good model. A few edges, said out loud so nobody thinks the policy is magic:

  • The branch manager sees customers of their home branch, including a card swipe that happened somewhere else. They do not see a walk-in whose home branch is elsewhere.

  • Dev schemas carry no policies (the post-hook runs in prod only) and no persona grants. The demo is the prod mart.

12.6 Policies and masking that survive a rebuild

  1. Declare intent on the model. meta: {governance: {row_access: {policy: RAP_CUSTOMER_SCOPE, column: customer_id}, pii: {email: contact, ...}}}. A reviewer sees the protection next to the SQL.
  2. Security owns the policy. 06 creates policies IF NOT EXISTS and changes their body with ALTER ROW ACCESS POLICY … SET BODY, because an attached policy cannot be replaced. It grants APPLY on them to the transformer: dbt may attach, never edit.
  3. dbt re-attaches on every build. CREATE OR REPLACE drops policies, so the prod post-hook apply_governance() drops any existing row policy, adds the declared one, and sets GOVERNANCE.PII tags on PII columns. dbt run-operation apply_governance_all does the same for every model without rebuilding.
  4. Fail closed. If 06 has not run, the hook logs a warning and the personas still have no access path, because there is no entitlement row for them. Nothing leaks while governance is missing.
  5. Tag-based masking. One masking policy per type (STRING, NUMBER(18,2), DATE) is attached to the tag, not to columns. Tagging a new column protects it. Column types must match the policy type — the one known risk, listed in the design doc.

SQL (12)
Why did my LEFT JOIN behave like an INNER JOIN?
A filter on the right table in WHERE removes the NULL-extended rows. Put it in ON. — L01.
ROW_NUMBER vs RANK vs DENSE_RANK?
Ties: ROW_NUMBER arbitrary unique; RANK skips (1,1,3); DENSE_RANK does not (1,1,2). — L04.
Why does NOT IN (subquery) return nothing?
One NULL in the list makes every comparison UNKNOWN. Use NOT EXISTS. — L07.
Running total and the frame clause?
SUM() OVER (PARTITION BY account ORDER BY ts ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW); default frame with ORDER BY is RANGE, which groups ties. — L03.
Forward-fill missing values in SQL?
Date spine × key, then LAST_VALUE(x IGNORE NULLS) OVER (... ROWS UNBOUNDED PRECEDING), or the COUNT-over-grouping trick in Postgres. — L06, int_fx_rates_daily_filled.
COUNT(*) vs COUNT(col) vs COUNT(DISTINCT col)?
All rows; non-null values; distinct non-null values. — L08.
What does QUALIFY do?
Filters on window-function output without a subquery. Dedupe idiom: qualify row_number() over (partition by pk order by ts desc) = 1. — every staging model.
EXISTS vs IN vs JOIN for semi-joins?
Semantically EXISTS/IN do not duplicate; JOIN can. Modern planners treat EXISTS and IN similarly; NOT IN has the NULL trap. — L07.
How do you read EXPLAIN ANALYZE?
Find the node with the biggest actual time; check Seq Scan vs Index Scan, "Rows Removed by Filter", estimated vs actual rows (stats), buffers hit vs read. — L10, perf lab 01.
Timestamp with vs without time zone?
TIMESTAMPTZ is an absolute instant (stored UTC, displayed per session); TIMESTAMP is wall-clock with no zone. Store instants, convert for display. — L09, extractor.
Recursive CTE use cases?
Hierarchies (org, accounts), graph walks, generating series where none exists. — L11.
Find duplicates / the latest row per key?
GROUP BY … HAVING COUNT(*) > 1; latest = window ROW_NUMBER or DISTINCT ON in Postgres.
Data modelling (12)
What is grain and why first?
The exact meaning of one fact row. Every dimension and measure must be true at that grain; mixing grains is the root of double counting. — M2.1.
Star vs snowflake schema?
Star: denormalised dims, fewer joins, BI-friendly. Snowflake: normalised dims, more joins, marginal storage saving. Columnar compression makes star the default. — M2.5.
SCD types 0–6 with an example each?
0 DOB; 1 branch name; 2 segment (new row); 3 previous segment column; 4 mini-dim for risk band; 6 = 1+2+3. — M2.3.
How do facts join to a Type 2 dimension?
At load time, pick the version where event_ts >= valid_from and < valid_to and store that version's surrogate key. — scd2_match(), fct_transactions.
Surrogate vs natural keys?
Surrogate: stable, version-specific, immune to source re-keying, compact joins. Keep the natural key as an attribute. — all dims.
Transaction vs periodic vs accumulating snapshot facts?
Event rows; one row per entity per period (semi-additive); one row per process instance updated as milestones occur. — fct_transactions, fct_monthly_account_balance, fct_loan_applications.
Additive, semi-additive, non-additive?
Amount; balance (not across time); rates/ratios. — M2.4.
Conformed dimension and drill-across?
Same dim shared by facts; lets you combine metrics from two facts by the shared attributes. — dim_branch.
Bridge table and weighting factor?
Resolves M:N (joint accounts); 1/n weights make allocated totals reconcile. — bridge_account_customer.
Junk, degenerate, role-playing dimensions?
Flags combined into one small dim; a key with no dim table (transaction_id); one dim in multiple roles (applied/approved date). — M2.5.
Late-arriving facts and dimensions?
Fact arrives after its period: incremental lookback + merge. Dimension arrives after the fact: unknown member now, repoint later. — M5.6.
Multi-currency in a warehouse?
Store local + reporting + rate on the fact; flows at transaction-date rate, stocks at period-end rate; fill rate gaps; never convert at query time. — M2.6.
Snowflake (16)
Architecture layers?
Cloud services, virtual warehouses (compute), centralised storage in micro-partitions. Compute and storage scale and bill separately. — M3.1.
Micro-partitions and pruning?
Immutable 50–500 MB columnar chunks with min/max metadata; the optimizer skips partitions whose range cannot match. — perf lab 02.
When to cluster and on what?
Multi-TB tables with a dominant filter column where natural order is poor; low-to-moderate cardinality expressions; measure with SYSTEM$CLUSTERING_INFORMATION. — perf lab 02.
Scale up vs scale out?
Up (bigger size) for slow/spilling queries; out (multi-cluster) for concurrency. — perf lab 03, 09.
The three caches?
Result cache (24h, exact SQL, no compute), warehouse local disk cache (until suspend), metadata cache (count/min/max). — perf lab 05.
What is spilling?
Operator working set exceeds memory → local SSD → remote storage; fix by rewriting or sizing up. — perf lab 03.
Time Travel vs Fail-safe vs clone?
TT: query/restore past states (0–90 days, you control). Fail-safe: 7 days, Snowflake support only. Clone: zero-copy snapshot of a table/schema/db. — 05_time_travel_clone_demo.sql.
How do you prove RPO and RTO?
Declare targets, inject a failure in a disposable environment, time detection and verified recovery, reconcile, and retain evidence. Time Travel is a mechanism; a timed runbook is the proof. — DR_RUNBOOK.md, 10_dr_exercises.sql.
Permanent vs transient vs temporary?
Fail-safe + TT up to 90 d; no Fail-safe, TT ≤ 1 d; session-scoped. Raw/staging → transient.
Loading options?
COPY (batch), Snowpipe (file events), Snowpipe Streaming (rows), Kafka connector, external/Iceberg tables. — M4.6.
Streams and Tasks?
Stream = change table (CDC offsets) on a table; Task = scheduled SQL/procedure, chained into DAGs, gated by SYSTEM$STREAM_HAS_DATA. — 04_streams_tasks…sql.
Dynamic Tables vs Streams+Tasks vs dbt?
DT: declarative, target lag, automatic incremental refresh. S+T: imperative, full control. dbt: testing, docs, versioning, multi-step modelling. — M3.5.
Search Optimization vs clustering vs MV?
SOS for point lookups on non-clustered columns; clustering for range scans; MV for a fixed aggregate on one table. All cost credits — measure. — perf lab 07.
RBAC model?
Privileges on objects granted to roles, roles to users/roles; ownership; future grants; functional vs access roles; service users with key-pair. — M3.2. Row access is the next step: the role can SELECT the table, and a policy still removes rows using CURRENT_USER(). — 06_row_level_security.sql.
How do you control cost?
Resource monitors, auto-suspend, right-sized warehouses per workload, statement timeouts, query tags, incremental builds, metering history reviews. — M8.4.
VARIANT: how is it stored and queried?
Columnarised sub-columns automatically when the shape is consistent; col:path::type; FLATTEN for arrays. — raw layer.

Loaded file names remembered 64 days; FORCE overrides; PURGE deletes after load; VALIDATION_MODE for dry runs. — M4.4.

Sources → staging (1:1, rename/cast) → intermediate (logic) → marts (star). Only staging touches source(). — M5.1.

merge (default, needs unique_key), delete+insert, append, microbatch (1.9+, per-period batches). — fct_transactions, agg_branch_daily_performance.

Resolves to the fully qualified name for the target and records the dependency edge that builds the DAG.
Slim CI, state, defer?
state:modified+ selects changed nodes and descendants vs a manifest; --defer reads unselected upstreams from prod. — M8.1.

Declared raw tables with loaded_at_field; dbt source freshness warns/errors on staleness. — M5.2.

Python & ingestion (8)
Design an incremental extractor; what can go wrong?
Watermark on updated_at; missed deletes (delete log), in-flight transactions (lag window), clock skew (max observed), schema drift (VARIANT), type drift (explicit Arrow schema), partial failure (watermark moves only on success). — M4.
Why Parquet?
Columnar, compressed, typed, splittable; Snowflake reads logical types directly; 5–10× smaller than CSV with no quoting problems.

Nullable ints → float; decimals → float; tz-aware timestamps; memory (use server-side cursors and chunks). — M4.3.
Idempotency?
Re-running produces the same result: append-only raw + dedupe in staging + watermark advanced transactionally.
Exactly-once vs at-least-once?
We deliver at-least-once and make consumers idempotent — the practical answer in data engineering.
Where does Python beat SQL/dbt here?
Moving data between systems, file formats, orchestration glue, cross-system reconciliation. Not for transformations inside the warehouse.

Architecture & system design (8)
What goes in a design doc?
Problem, functional requirements, NFRs as measurable targets, architecture, the decisions with rejected alternatives, capacity and cost arithmetic, data classification, failure modes, what was deliberately left out, open risks. — 00_architecture/DESIGN_DOC.md.
What is an ADR and why bother?
A short, immutable record of one hard-to-reverse decision: context, decision, alternatives, consequences, when to revisit. Stops the next engineer "fixing" a deliberate choice. — adr/.
Estimate the monthly cost of a 5-minute micro-batch.
288 runs × (run time + auto-suspend idle) per day, converted to warehouse-hours × credits/hour × price. ~360 credits/month here. Then say which tables do not need that cadence. — design doc §4.
Kimball vs Data Vault vs one big table?
Kimball for consumption and semantic layers; Data Vault when many sources integrate the same keys and load auditability is paramount; OBT for one fixed dashboard. — ADR 0003.
When would you adopt Iceberg?
When a second engine needs the same data, data must sit in your own storage in an open format, or egress/storage cost justifies it. Not before. — ADR 0007.
Watermark vs log-based CDC?
Watermark: simple, misses trigger-bypassing updates and intermediate states. Log-based: complete, needs replication slots and a connector runtime. Start simple, keep the raw shape so the upgrade does not touch staging. — ADR 0001.
What would you change at 10× volume?
Log-based CDC or Snowpipe Streaming for hot tables, Dynamic Tables for the fraud path, reference tables off the hot path, per-domain databases with database roles, Iceberg for raw if a second engine appears.

Context (C4 level 1), after the requirements and before any tool. Then a conceptual model (ANSI/SPARC), then the Kimball bus matrix, then containers and a data flow, then ADRs, then the physical model. — ARCHITECTURE_PROCESS.md.
A user wants to type part of a transaction id and search the whole platform. What do you build?
A serving index, one row per identifier, Search Optimization on SUBSTRING(search_text), row access policy on that table. Not a LIKE across every mart, and not a second search engine, until the thing being searched is free text. — ADR 0009, omni_search.
Who owns which Snowflake objects?
Terraform: platform. dbt: data objects and their grants. Security: policies and entitlements. IdP: users. One owner per object type. — ADR 0008.
Governance & security (7)
Row access policy vs secure view vs filter in the app?
The policy is attached to the table and holds for every client. Secure views multiply per persona and drift. App filters are bypassed by any other client, including an LLM writing SQL. — ADR 0006.
Why CURRENT_USER() and not CURRENT_ROLE() in Streamlit in Snowflake?
The app runs with its owner's role for every viewer. With READ SESSION, CURRENT_USER() is the viewer. — 06_row_level_security.sql.
How do policies survive CREATE OR REPLACE?
A post-hook re-attaches them from model meta; dbt holds APPLY, not ownership; policy bodies change via SET BODY. — macros/governance.sql.
Column masking vs tag-based masking?
Column: attach a policy to each column. Tag: attach the policy to a tag once; tagging a column protects it. Scales with schema growth; types must match. — M12.6.
What does deny by default mean here?
A user not in APP_USER gets zero rows; only named engineering roles bypass. The app's owner role is not one of them.
How do you prove who saw PII?
ACCOUNT_USAGE.ACCESS_HISTORY filtered to PII-tagged columns, by user and time. — end of 06.
Where should passwords live?
Not in the repo. Humans: SSO/MFA, or set in the UI with must-change. Services: key-pair, keys in a secret manager, rotated through two public-key slots.
ML engineering (6)
What is training/serving skew and how did you prevent it?
Scoring on features computed differently from training. One dbt feature model (ml_customer_features) is read by both. — ADR 0005.
Why a time-based split?
A random split lets the model see the future. Holding out the latest 60 days of applications mimics deployment. — time_split.
How do you stop label leakage?
An explicit forbidden-feature list checked before training and in pytest; features frozen at application time. — assert_no_leakage.
What stops a bad model reaching production?
A holdout metric gate; the run is recorded as REJECTED and the marts keep reading the last passing run. — MODEL_RUNS, stg_ml__model_scores.
Why not write scores back to the source database?
Analytical output in the system of record, a replication loop, and coupling the OLTP to model releases. Operational consumers get an API or reverse ETL with a contract.
Rules or a model for fraud?
Rules are explainable and immediate; the model ranks within them. Measure rules by pattern recall and precision; never use the injected label as a feature.

E. Glossary

CDC
Change data capture: identifying rows that changed in the source.
Watermark
The highest change timestamp already processed.
Micro-partition
Snowflake's immutable storage unit with per-column metadata.
Pruning
Skipping micro-partitions using metadata.
Spilling
Writing intermediate results to disk when memory is exhausted.
Grain
What one fact row represents.
Conformed dimension
A dimension shared, identically, by multiple facts.
SCD2
History kept as versioned rows with validity ranges.
PIT key
Point-in-time surrogate key: the dim version valid when the event happened.
Semi-additive
Summable across some dimensions but not time (balances).
Idempotent
Running again produces the same end state.
Slim CI
Building only changed models and descendants against a saved manifest.
Defer
Resolving unbuilt refs to another environment's objects.
Exposure
A declared downstream consumer in dbt lineage.
Dynamic Table
Snowflake declarative, auto-refreshed table with a target lag.
SOS
Search Optimization Service: persistent access path for selective lookups.
ADR
Architecture Decision Record: one significant decision, its alternatives and its cost, never edited after the fact.
NFR / SLO
Non-functional requirement; service level objective — a measurable target such as "p95 freshness ≤ 15 minutes".
Model contract
A dbt-enforced promise of a model's column names and types; the build fails before consumers break.
Row access policy
A Snowflake function attached to a table that decides, per row and per query, whether the caller may see it.
Training/serving skew
A model scored on features computed differently from the ones it was trained on.
